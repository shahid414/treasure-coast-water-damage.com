Local Causes

What Causes Water Damage in Jensen Beach

Life between the Atlantic and the Indian River Lagoon means Jensen Beach homes meet water from more directions than most. These are the sources behind most of the calls we run here:

Storm surge & wind-driven rain

Hutchinson Island takes the first hit when tropical systems cross the coast. Surge and sideways rain find their way under sliding doors, through window tracks and into ground-floor condos along Ocean Drive.

Condo stack & shared-wall leaks

In beachside condo buildings, one unit's failed water heater or AC line becomes the ceiling stain in the unit below. We trace the true source and document it for owners and associations alike.

Aging beachside plumbing

Many of Jensen Beach's older cottages and condos still run on original supply lines and cast-iron drains. Salt air accelerates corrosion, and pinhole leaks hide inside walls for months.

AC condensate overflows

Humidity off the lagoon keeps AC systems working hard. A clogged condensate line in a closet air handler quietly soaks drywall and flooring before anyone notices a drip.

Vacation rental & seasonal-home leaks

Units that sit empty between guests and snowbird seasons can leak for days before a neighbor or cleaner spots it. By then, one room of water has become a whole-unit drying job.

Slab & appliance leaks

Mainland subdivisions off Jensen Beach Boulevard and Savanna Road sit on slabs with lines beneath. A failed washing-machine hose or slab pinhole spreads under tile and laminate fast.

Act Fast

When to Call — and What to Do While You Wait

Call right away if you see:

  • Water is still moving or spreading across the floor
  • Standing water in any room, garage or ground-floor condo
  • Walls, ceilings or baseboards feel wet, soft or swollen
  • A musty smell that was not there yesterday
  • A ceiling stain or drip from the unit or floor above
  • Storm water pushed in under doors or through the roof

While you wait for the crew:

  1. Stop the source if you can do it safely — close the fixture valve, the unit shutoff, or the main.
  2. Cut power to wet areas at the breaker if water is near outlets or appliances.
  3. Photograph everything before moving it — your insurer (and possibly your condo association) will want a record.
  4. Move valuables to dry ground; lift furniture legs onto blocks or foil if you can.
  5. Call before you clean. In salt air, wet materials corrode and mold faster — extraction and drying in the first hours prevent most of it.

The Full Job

What a Complete Water Damage Job Includes

Restoration is not one task — it is four phases in the right order. Skipping a phase is how moisture gets trapped behind new drywall. Here is how every job runs:

1 · Removal

Standing water is extracted and materials that cannot be saved — soaked pad, swollen particleboard, saturated insulation — come out before they feed mold.

2 · Cleanup

Debris is cleared, surfaces are cleaned and treated, and contaminated water (especially sewage and surge water) is handled with protective equipment and proper disposal.

3 · Drying

Air movers and commercial dehumidifiers pull moisture out of walls, cavities and subfloors until meters confirm a safe dry standard.

4 · Repair

Drywall, flooring, cabinets, trim and paint are restored under a written scope you approve before the rebuild starts.

Know Your Water

The Three Categories of Water Damage

Not all water is handled the same way. The category decides what can be dried in place, what has to come out, and what safety steps apply:

Category 1 — Clean Water

From a supply line, tub overflow or appliance hose. It starts clean, but it does not stay that way: once it sits in flooring and wall cavities it picks up contaminants, so fast extraction still matters.

Category 2 — Gray Water

From washing machines, dishwashers, sump failures or toilet overflows without solids. It carries detergents and bacteria — porous materials it soaks usually cannot be saved.

Category 3 — Black Water

Sewage backups, storm surge and floodwater from outside — a real concern on Hutchinson Island. Treat it as a health hazard: keep people and pets out, do not run fans through it, and let a crew with protective equipment handle it.
