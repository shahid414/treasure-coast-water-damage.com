Local Causes

What Causes Water Damage in Port Salerno

Port Salerno grew up as a working fishing village on the Manatee Pocket, and its homes — old and new — sit close to the water. These are the sources behind most of the calls we run here:

Waterfront & pocket flooding

Low-lying streets around the Manatee Pocket and the St. Lucie River flood first when heavy rain meets a high tide. Water under doors and into garages is a recurring call after summer storms.

Aging plumbing in historic cottages

Many of Port Salerno's older cottages still have original galvanized or cast-iron lines. Corrosion pinholes and slow drain leaks soak subfloors and wall bottoms for months before showing.

Hurricane wind-driven rain

Tropical systems crossing the Treasure Coast push rain through roof vents, window seals and sliding doors — especially in older homes that have weathered many storms already.

AC condensate overflows

Humid air off the water keeps AC systems running hard. A clogged condensate line in a closet or attic air handler drips into ceilings and walls long before a stain appears.

Slab leaks & appliance failures

Newer homes along Salerno Road sit on slabs with lines beneath. A pinhole slab leak or a failed water-heater in the garage spreads under tile and laminate quickly.

Sewage & drain backups

Older drain lines and heavy rain are a bad combination. Backups through floor drains and toilets are a health hazard that needs professional cleanup, not a mop.

Act Fast

When to Call — and What to Do While You Wait

Call right away if you see:

  • Water is still moving or spreading across the floor
  • Standing water in any room, garage or crawl space
  • Walls, ceilings or baseboards feel wet, soft or swollen
  • A musty smell that was not there yesterday
  • Water backing up through a floor drain or toilet
  • Storm or tide water pushed in under doors

While you wait for the crew:

  1. Stop the source if you can do it safely — close the fixture valve or the main shutoff.
  2. Cut power to wet areas at the breaker if water is near outlets or appliances.
  3. Photograph everything before moving it — your insurer will want a record.
  4. Move valuables to dry ground; lift furniture legs onto blocks or foil if you can.
  5. Call before you clean. Older Port Salerno homes trap moisture in subfloors and wall bottoms — early extraction and drying prevent most mold.

The Full Job

What a Complete Water Damage Restoration Job Includes

Restoration is not one task — it is four phases in the right order. Skipping a phase is how moisture gets trapped behind new drywall. Here is how every job runs:

1 · Removal

Standing water is extracted and materials that cannot be saved — soaked pad, swollen particleboard, saturated insulation — come out before they feed mold.

2 · Cleanup

Debris is cleared, surfaces are cleaned and treated, and contaminated water (especially sewage) is handled with protective equipment and proper disposal.

3 · Drying

Air movers and commercial dehumidifiers pull moisture out of walls, cavities and subfloors until meters confirm a safe dry standard.

4 · Repair

Drywall, flooring, cabinets, trim and paint are restored under a written scope you approve before the rebuild starts.

Know Your Water

The Three Categories of Water Damage

Not all water is handled the same way. The category decides what can be dried in place, what has to come out, and what safety steps apply:

Category 1 — Clean Water

From a supply line, tub overflow or appliance hose. It starts clean, but it does not stay that way: once it sits in flooring and wall cavities it picks up contaminants, so fast extraction still matters.

Category 2 — Gray Water

From washing machines, dishwashers, sump failures or toilet overflows without solids. It carries detergents and bacteria — porous materials it soaks usually cannot be saved.

Category 3 — Black Water

Sewage backups, storm surge and floodwater from outside, including river and pocket water. Treat it as a health hazard: keep people and pets out, do not run fans through it, and let a crew with protective equipment handle it.
