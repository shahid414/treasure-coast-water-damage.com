Local Causes

What Causes Water Damage in Hobe Sound

Hobe Sound sits between the Atlantic and the Loxahatchee River, with older established neighborhoods and newer coastal homes side by side. These are the sources behind most of the calls we run here:

Coastal storm & surge exposure

Homes near the beach and Jupiter Island take wind-driven rain and, in stronger storms, surge water under doors and into ground floors. Saltwater needs fast, professional handling — it corrodes and contaminates as it sits.

Aging plumbing in established neighborhoods

Many Hobe Sound homes built decades ago still run on original supply and drain lines. Corrosion pinholes and slow leaks hide in walls and under floors until the damage is well advanced.

AC condensate overflows

Coastal humidity keeps air handlers working hard most of the year. A clogged condensate line quietly soaks ceilings, closets and wall cavities — often in homes that sit empty between visits.

Seasonal & second-home leaks

Many Hobe Sound properties are second homes. A failed water heater or supply line in an empty house can run for days before a neighbor, caretaker or housekeeper finds it.

Slab leaks

Homes on slabs with copper lines beneath announce pinhole leaks as warm spots on tile, buckling floors or a water bill that jumps for no reason.

Summer downpour flooding

Heavy afternoon cells overwhelm drainage in low areas near the river and Peck Lake. Garages and lanais take the first water, and it wicks into walls from there.

Act Fast

When to Call — and What to Do While You Wait

Call right away if you see:

  • Water is still moving or spreading across the floor
  • Standing water in any room, garage or crawl space
  • Walls, ceilings or baseboards feel wet, soft or swollen
  • A musty smell that was not there yesterday
  • AC or appliance leaks dripping into ceilings or cabinets
  • Storm or surge water pushed in under doors

While you wait for the crew:

  1. Stop the source if you can do it safely — close the fixture valve or the main shutoff.
  2. Cut power to wet areas at the breaker if water is near outlets or appliances.
  3. Photograph everything before moving it — your insurer will want a record.
  4. Move valuables to dry ground; lift furniture legs onto blocks or foil if you can.
  5. Call before you clean. In coastal humidity, mold can start within 24 to 48 hours — early extraction and drying prevent most of it.

The Full Job

What a Complete Water Damage Restoration Job Includes

Restoration is not one task — it is four phases in the right order. Skipping a phase is how moisture gets trapped behind new drywall. Here is how every job runs:

1 · Removal

Standing water is extracted and materials that cannot be saved — soaked pad, swollen particleboard, saturated insulation — come out before they feed mold.

2 · Cleanup

Debris is cleared, surfaces are cleaned and treated, and contaminated water (especially sewage and surge water) is handled with protective equipment and proper disposal.

3 · Drying

Air movers and commercial dehumidifiers pull moisture out of walls, cavities and subfloors until meters confirm a safe dry standard.

4 · Repair

Drywall, flooring, cabinets, trim and paint are restored under a written scope you approve before the rebuild starts.

Know Your Water

The Three Categories of Water Damage

Not all water is handled the same way. The category decides what can be dried in place, what has to come out, and what safety steps apply:

Category 1 — Clean Water

From a supply line, tub overflow or appliance hose. It starts clean, but it does not stay that way: once it sits in flooring and wall cavities it picks up contaminants, so fast extraction still matters.

Category 2 — Gray Water

From washing machines, dishwashers, sump failures or toilet overflows without solids. It carries detergents and bacteria — porous materials it soaks usually cannot be saved.

Category 3 — Black Water

Sewage backups, storm surge and floodwater from outside. Treat it as a health hazard: keep people and pets out, do not run fans through it, and let a crew with protective equipment handle it.
