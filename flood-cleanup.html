What Flood Cleanup Actually Involves

"Flood cleanup" sounds simple until you are standing in it. A flooded Stuart home usually means several problems at once: standing water that keeps soaking into everything it touches, debris and ruined materials that have to come out, surfaces that need sanitizing because flood water is rarely clean, and hidden moisture in walls and floors that will grow mold if it is not dried properly. Our flood cleanup service handles all four under one written scope, in the right order.

The sequence matters. Extracting water before removing debris just spreads contamination around; drying before sanitizing can blow contaminants through the home. We follow the same order the restoration industry uses: make the area safe, extract the water, remove what cannot be saved, clean and sanitize, then dry the structure to a verified standard.

Our Flood Cleanup Process

  1. Safety first. We check for electrical hazards, sagging ceilings and contamination before anyone starts working. Sewage-contaminated water gets full protective equipment and a different protocol — see sewage cleanup.
  2. Water extraction. Pumps and extractors remove standing water from every affected room, including garages and low spots where water collects. See emergency water extraction.
  3. Debris and material removal. Saturated carpet pad, ruined drywall, swollen baseboards and unsalvageable contents come out and are disposed of properly. Everything removed is photographed and listed for your insurance claim.
  4. Cleaning and sanitizing. Hard surfaces are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products appropriate to the contamination level, and odor sources are addressed at the root, not masked.
  5. Structural drying. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the remaining structure, verified daily with moisture meters. See structural drying.
  6. Repairs. Drywall, flooring, cabinets and paint are restored under a written scope you approve. A single contained room typically runs $500 to $2,500 after drying.

Common Flood Sources in Stuart Homes

  • Appliance failures — washing-machine hoses, dishwasher leaks and refrigerator supply lines that run for hours before anyone notices.
  • Burst pipes and water heaters — especially in older downtown Stuart homes with aging plumbing.
  • Storm flooding — heavy Treasure Coast rain, overwhelmed drainage and wind-driven water entering through doors, windows and roofs.
  • Overflowing fixtures — upstairs tubs and toilets that send water through ceilings into rooms below.

Whatever the source, the cleanup standard is the same: no standing water, no contaminated materials left behind, no hidden moisture, and documentation that shows exactly what was done.

What Flood Cleanup Costs

Flood cleanup in Stuart typically runs $2,500 to $9,000, with the final number driven by the square footage affected, the contamination category of the water, how much debris and material must be removed, and how many days of drying are required. Clean-water losses from a supply line are at the lower end; storm flooding with silt and debris, or any loss involving sewage, sits higher. Your inspection is free and your quote is itemized in writing before work begins — see our cost guide for the full breakdown.

Homeowners insurance often covers sudden, accidental water damage from inside the home; flooding from outside surface water typically requires separate flood insurance. Either way, we document the loss thoroughly — photos, moisture logs and an itemized scope — so your claim file is complete.

What To Do Before We Arrive

  • Stay out of standing water if it may be electrically charged or sewage-contaminated.
  • Shut off the water source and, if safe, the power to affected areas.
  • Photograph everything before moving it — wide shots and close-ups.
  • Move dry belongings and documents to higher ground; do not throw away damaged items until they are documented.
  • Call us at (772) 555-0100 — we will talk you through the next steps while a crew is dispatched.
