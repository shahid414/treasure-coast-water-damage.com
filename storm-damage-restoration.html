How Storms Damage Stuart Homes

Stuart sits in one of the most storm-exposed stretches of Florida's east coast, and storm water rarely arrives through just one opening. Wind-driven rain forces its way under roof edges, through window and door seals, and into soffits and wall cavities. Heavy rainfall overwhelms street drainage and pushes surface water toward slabs and doorways. Fallen branches and wind-blown debris can breach roofs and windows outright. The result is often a home that is wet in several places at once — ceilings, walls, floors and contents — with silt and debris carried in by the water.

Storm losses also come with a clock. After a major storm, restoration companies across Martin and St. Lucie counties are flooded with calls, and the homes that get dried first are the ones that avoid mold and full tear-outs. Calling early — even before you have finished assessing the damage — puts your home in line for extraction and drying equipment while it can still save your floors and walls.

Our Storm Restoration Process

  1. Emergency response. We answer 24/7, including during and immediately after storms when conditions allow safe travel. On the call we help you shut off water and power to damaged areas and confirm an arrival window.
  2. Extraction and debris removal. Standing water is pumped and extracted, storm debris and unsalvageable wet materials are removed, and everything is photographed before and after for your claim. See emergency water extraction and flood cleanup.
  3. Moisture mapping. Storm water hides in wall cavities, under flooring and above ceilings. We scan with moisture meters and thermal imaging so nothing wet is left behind to grow mold.
  4. Structural drying. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure to a verified dry standard, with daily readings documented. See structural drying.
  5. Repairs and rebuild. Drywall, flooring, cabinets, trim and paint are restored under a written, itemized scope you approve before work begins.

Insurance Documentation After a Storm

Storm claims live or die on documentation. Adjusters need to see what was damaged, what was wet, what was removed and why — ideally documented before anything is thrown away. On every storm job we photograph the damage on arrival, record moisture readings room by room, log equipment and drying progress daily, and itemize the repair scope. That file gives your insurer a clear picture of the loss and gives you a record of exactly what was done.

A practical note on coverage: standard homeowners policies generally cover wind-driven rain that enters through storm-created openings, while flooding from rising surface water typically requires separate flood insurance. We cannot decide coverage for you, but complete documentation helps either claim. If you are unsure what your policy covers, call your insurer early — and keep every photo and invoice.

What Storm Damage Restoration Costs

Storm flood cleanup in Stuart typically runs $2,500 to $9,000 for the mitigation phase — extraction, debris removal, sanitizing and drying — with repairs priced separately in the written scope (a single contained room after drying typically runs $500 to $2,500). The main cost drivers are the number of rooms affected, how much debris and material must come out, and how many days of drying are needed. Your inspection is free and every number is itemized before work starts. See our cost guide for details.

After the Storm: First Steps

  • Wait until conditions are safe — avoid downed power lines and standing water near electrical equipment.
  • Photograph all damage before moving or discarding anything.
  • Shut off water and power to damaged areas if you can do so safely.
  • Move dry belongings away from wet areas; lift fabrics off wet floors.
  • Call your insurer to open a claim, then call us at (772) 555-0100 so mitigation can start while the claim is processed — insurers expect you to prevent further damage.
