What Drives the Final Price?

1. How much water, and how long it sat

A supply-line leak caught within the hour is a different job from the same leak running all weekend. The longer materials stay wet, the more must be removed instead of dried, and the longer drying takes. Fast response is the single biggest cost saver.

2. The category of water

Clean water from a supply pipe is the least expensive to handle. Gray water from appliances costs more. Sewage and outside flood water (Category 3) require protective equipment, more material removal and full disinfection — and are priced accordingly.

3. Materials involved

Tile and concrete dry quickly. Carpet and pad can sometimes be saved with specialty extraction. Hardwood, laminate, drywall and particleboard cabinets absorb water and may need removal. Hardwood and tile-over-mortar assemblies also dry slowly, which adds equipment days.

4. Square footage and access

More rooms means more equipment, more labor and more monitoring visits. Tight spaces — under cabinets, inside wall cavities, above ceilings — take longer to dry and verify.

Does Insurance Cover Water Damage Restoration?

Often, yes — with important limits. Sudden and accidental water damage from inside the home (burst pipes, appliance failures) is commonly covered by homeowners insurance. Long-term leaks, maintenance issues and flooding from outside surface water usually are not, and mold coverage is frequently limited unless it follows a covered water loss. Sewage backup is often covered only through a specific endorsement with its own limit.

We cannot promise what your policy will pay — that is your insurer's decision. What we can do is document the loss the way adjusters expect: dated photos, room-by-room moisture readings, an itemized scope, and records of everything removed and why. That documentation protects you whether the claim is approved in full, in part, or paid out of pocket.

How to Keep Costs Down

  • Shut off the water source immediately — every gallon stopped is damage prevented.
  • Call for extraction the same day; waiting a weekend can double the scope.
  • Do not throw away damaged items before they are photographed and documented.
  • Dry professionally and verify with meters — hidden moisture left behind becomes mold remediation later.
  • Ask for the written scope before approving repairs, and keep every invoice for your claim.
