Local Causes

What Causes Water Damage in Palm City

Palm City mixes riverfront estates, gated golf communities and working horse properties — and each floods a little differently. These are the sources behind most of the calls we run here:

Riverfront & canal flooding

Homes along the St. Lucie River and its canals sit low by design. Heavy rain bands and high tides push water into garages, lanais and first floors of waterfront properties.

AC condensate overflows

Large Palm City homes run multiple air handlers, often in attic or closet spaces. A clogged condensate line can rain into ceilings and custom cabinetry before a stain ever shows.

Slab leaks in newer subdivisions

Gated communities built in the last few decades sit on slabs with copper lines beneath. Pinhole leaks show up as warm tile, cupping wood floors or a water meter that never stops.

Burst pipes & water heaters

Garage water heaters and aging supply lines are steady culprits across Palm City. In larger floor plans, a failed heater can flood several rooms before anyone walks in.

Barn & outbuilding plumbing

Equestrian properties run water lines to barns, wash racks and guest houses. A break in an outbuilding line can soak tack rooms and feed storage — and run unnoticed for days.

Hurricane wind-driven rain

Tropical systems push rain through tile-roof valleys, window seals and sliding doors across Palm City's subdivisions, soaking attics and second-story ceilings.

Act Fast

When to Call — and What to Do While You Wait

Call right away if you see:

  • Water is still moving or spreading across the floor
  • Standing water in any room, garage or barn
  • Walls, ceilings or baseboards feel wet, soft or swollen
  • Wood floors cupping, buckling or separating
  • A musty smell that was not there yesterday
  • Storm or river water pushed in under doors

While you wait for the crew:

  1. Stop the source if you can do it safely — close the fixture valve, the outbuilding shutoff, or the main.
  2. Cut power to wet areas at the breaker if water is near outlets or appliances.
  3. Photograph everything before moving it — your insurer will want a record.
  4. Move valuables to dry ground; lift furniture legs onto blocks or foil if you can.
  5. Call before you clean. Wood floors and custom finishes in Palm City homes are saved or lost in the first hours of drying.

The Full Job

What a Complete Water Damage Job Includes

Restoration is not one task — it is four phases in the right order. Skipping a phase is how moisture gets trapped behind new drywall. Here is how every job runs:

1 · Removal

Standing water is extracted and materials that cannot be saved — soaked pad, swollen particleboard, saturated insulation — come out before they feed mold.

2 · Cleanup

Debris is cleared, surfaces are cleaned and treated, and contaminated water (especially sewage) is handled with protective equipment and proper disposal.

3 · Drying

Air movers and commercial dehumidifiers pull moisture out of walls, cavities and subfloors until meters confirm a safe dry standard.

4 · Repair

Drywall, flooring, cabinets, trim and paint are restored under a written scope you approve before the rebuild starts.

Know Your Water

The Three Categories of Water Damage

Not all water is handled the same way. The category decides what can be dried in place, what has to come out, and what safety steps apply:

Category 1 — Clean Water

From a supply line, tub overflow or appliance hose. It starts clean, but it does not stay that way: once it sits in flooring and wall cavities it picks up contaminants, so fast extraction still matters.

Category 2 — Gray Water

From washing machines, dishwashers, sump failures or toilet overflows without solids. It carries detergents and bacteria — porous materials it soaks usually cannot be saved.

Category 3 — Black Water

Sewage backups, storm surge and floodwater from outside, including river and canal water. Treat it as a health hazard: keep people and pets out, do not run fans through it, and let a crew with protective equipment handle it.
